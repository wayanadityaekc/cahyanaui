Uluwatu & the Bukit Peninsula

Dramatic limestone cliffs, world-class surf, and a clifftop sunset temple.

Bali Guide › About the Island

The Bukit Peninsula, at Bali's southern tip, is a world of dramatic limestone cliffs, hidden coves, and some of the best surf on the planet. Uluwatu is its most famous corner.

Perched on the cliffs is the ancient Uluwatu sea temple, where a sunset Kecak fire dance is performed each evening above the crashing ocean. Nearby, beaches like Padang Padang and Melasti sit at the foot of soaring white cliffs.

It's a favourite for surfers, sunset-chasers, and anyone after that classic clifftop Bali view.

Visiting the Bukit

The Bukit is spread out, so it helps to pick a couple of spots rather than rushing the whole peninsula. Aim to reach the temple in the late afternoon: walk the clifftop path, watch the surf far below, then stay for the sunset Kecak performance in the amphitheatre beside it.

Keep an eye on your belongings around the temple, where the resident monkeys are known to snatch sunglasses and phones. The beaches here sit at the foot of steep stairways, so wear something you can walk down in.
