Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary

The Sacred Monkey Forest is a temple sanctuary in Padangtegal, central Ubud, run by the local village. Set across shaded jungle around three 14th-century temples, it is home to hundreds of Balinese long-tailed macaques that roam freely among visitors.

About the Monkey Forest

The Macaques

Balinese Long-Tailed Macaques

The sanctuary is home to hundreds of Balinese long-tailed macaques, living in several troops across the forest. They roam freely and are used to visitors, so you'll see them grooming, playing, and carrying their young along the paths and walls. They are wild animals and quick to grab loose food, sunglasses, or dangling bags, so your guide shows you how to keep your belongings safe and move among them calmly.

The Temples

Three 14th-Century Temples

The forest is a spiritual site as much as a monkey attraction, holding three temples dating from around the 14th century and still used by the Padangtegal community. Moss-covered stone guardians, dragon staircases, and weathered carvings sit among the trees. The main temple, Pura Dalem Agung, is reserved for worshippers, so visitors take it in from the paths.

The Forest

A Cool, Green Walk in Central Ubud

Towering banyan trees, hanging roots, and a mossy ravine make the forest feel far wilder than its location, a few minutes' walk from Ubud's main street. Shaded stone paths wind past a stream and over little bridges, cool even in the midday heat. Mornings are quieter and the monkeys more active, and the entrance fee is a little higher at weekends.
