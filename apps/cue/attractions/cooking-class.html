Balinese Cooking Class

Start at the morning market, end at a family table - grind your own spice paste, cook a full Balinese meal over an open flame, and eat what you made, the way it's done in an Ubud home.

The Experience

Step 1

The Morning Market

We start early at a working local market - not a tourist one. Your host walks you past the galangal, turmeric root, candlenut, lemongrass, and palm sugar, explaining what each one does and how to pick it. You'll learn more about Balinese food in this half hour than in a week of restaurants.

Step 2

Base Gede - The Spice Paste

Everything in Balinese cooking starts with base gede, the master spice paste. You'll grind it yourself in a stone mortar - no blender, no shortcuts - and understand immediately why the flavour is so different from anything you get from a jar. Your arms will ache. It's part of the process.

Step 3

Cook It, Then Eat It

You'll cook a full spread - usually sate lilit, lawar, tempeh in sweet soy, a vegetable urab, and a chicken or fish dish, all over an open flame in a family compound kitchen. Then you sit down and eat everything you made, together. Vegetarian and vegan versions are no problem - just tell us when you book.
