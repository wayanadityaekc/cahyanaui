Bali's Volcanoes: Mount Agung & Mount Batur

The sacred peaks that shape the island - and a bucket-list sunrise trek.

Bali Guide › Nature

Bali is a volcanic island, and its peaks shape both the landscape and the culture. Towering over the east is Mount Agung, the highest and most sacred volcano, believed to be the home of the gods and the point Balinese orient their temples toward.

Further north-west, Mount Batur is lower and gentler - an active volcano sitting inside a vast caldera beside a crater lake. Its pre-dawn hike is one of Bali's signature experiences.

The Batur sunrise trek is achievable for most reasonably fit walkers, rewarding you with the sun rising over the caldera and, on clear days, Agung in the distance.

Trekking the Volcanoes

The classic Batur trek starts in the small hours so you reach the summit before dawn; a local guide is required, and the route is a steady climb rather than a technical scramble. Bring a light jacket - the pre-dawn air at altitude is genuinely cold - along with sturdy shoes and a torch.

Mount Agung is a far more serious undertaking, reserved for experienced trekkers, and access can close during ceremonies or volcanic activity. Whichever peak you choose, both are sacred, so trek with a guide and follow their lead.
