Bali's Rice Terraces & the Subak System

Sculpted green landscapes farmed the same way for a thousand years.

Bali Guide › Nature

Bali's emerald rice terraces are among its most iconic sights, carved into hillsides over many generations. What makes them special isn't just the view - it's the subak, a thousand-year-old cooperative that shares irrigation water fairly between farmers.

Recognised by UNESCO, subak reflects the same Tri Hita Karana philosophy of harmony that runs through Balinese life. The terraces are still working farms, tended largely by hand.

Tegalalang, north of Ubud, is the most photographed; Jatiluwih, in the west, offers a vast, quiet UNESCO landscape with walking trails through the paddies.

Visiting the Terraces

The terraces are at their greenest a few months into the rainy season and again just before harvest, when the stalks turn gold. Early morning brings soft light and cooler air, and you'll beat both the heat and the busloads at popular spots like Tegalalang.

A small donation is often asked at village entrances, and some viewpoints have swings or photo platforms for a fee. Wear shoes with grip if you plan to walk down between the paddies - the paths are narrow and can be slippery after rain.
