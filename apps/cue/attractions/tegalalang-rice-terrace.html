Tegalalang Rice Terrace & Coffee Plantation

Bali's iconic stepped rice fields and a working coffee plantation, just north of Ubud.

About

The Tegalalang rice terraces are among Bali's most photographed landscapes - steep, sculpted paddies carved into the valley over generations and still watered by the island's thousand-year-old subak irrigation system, recognised by UNESCO. Right beside the fields, a working plantation grows coffee, cacao, and spices, where you can walk the grounds and taste a full flight of local brews - including the famous kopi luwak - straight from the source.

What makes it special is that it's still a living farm, not just a viewpoint. Local families tend the slopes by hand and share water through the communal subak system that has shaped Balinese life for centuries. Arrive early and you'll often catch the morning mist lifting off the valley while workers move between the paddies - one of those quiet moments that stays with you long after the trip.

Highlights

  • Dramatic terraced rice fields carved into the valley
  • Bali's ancient subak (UNESCO) irrigation still in use
  • Walk a working coffee, cacao & spice plantation
  • Complimentary tasting flight - herbal teas, coffees & kopi luwak
  • Great photo spots; optional jungle swing right next door

Good to Know

  • Wear comfortable shoes - some steps and uneven paths
  • A small entrance or donation may apply at certain viewpoints
  • Softest light is early morning; bring water & sun protection
