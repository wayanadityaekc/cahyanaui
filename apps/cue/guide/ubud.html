Ubud - Bali's Cultural Heart

Inland, green, and the island's centre of art, temples, and rice terraces.

Bali Guide › About the Island

Ubud is Bali's cultural capital, set in the hills at the centre of the island - about an hour from the airport and a world away from the beach towns. It's where the island's painting, carving, and dance traditions are concentrated, and it's surrounded on every side by the green that Bali is famous for: rice terraces, river valleys, and forest. It's also where we live and work, so consider this the local's introduction.

Getting Your Bearings

The centre is compact. Jalan Raya Ubud, the main road, runs past the Ubud Palace and the art market, which face each other in the middle of town. Monkey Forest Road runs south from there, lined with shops and restaurants, down to the forest itself. West across the river, Campuhan and Penestanan are quieter and greener; north and east, the town dissolves into villages and paddies within minutes. You can walk the core in half a day - pavements are narrow and uneven, so wear proper footwear.

What to Do in Town

Start early at the art market, which is at its best before the tour buses, and cross the road to the Ubud Palace, still home to Ubud's royal family. The Sacred Monkey Forest is a short walk south - a real temple sanctuary, not a petting zoo, so keep bags zipped and don't carry food.

For a quiet hour, the Campuhan Ridge Walk is a paved path along a ridgeline of tall grass - free, easy, and best before 9 AM or near sunset, when the light is soft and the heat is off. Art lovers have three serious museums within a short drive: Puri Lukisan, Neka, and ARMA, all built around Balinese painting. In the evening, a traditional dance is performed somewhere in town nearly every night; the Ubud Kecak runs Tuesdays and Sundays at 7 PM.

Around Ubud

The countryside is the point. The Tegalalang rice terraces are 20 minutes north; the craft villages - silver in Celuk, painting in Batuan, woodcarving in Mas - line the road south. The holy springs of Tirta Empul and the carved cliffs of Gunung Kawi make a good half-day east.

For something more active, the Ayung River rafting and the jungle swing both start minutes from town. Our Ubud Tour strings the highlights into one day; the Culture Day does the same for the arts side.

Eating in Ubud

Ubud eats well at every price. Warungs serve nasi campur and babi guling (Ubud's famous roast pork) for a few dollars; the cafe scene does everything from smoothie bowls to serious coffee; and a handful of restaurants are destinations in their own right. A good rule: one warung meal a day keeps both your budget and your cooking-class inspiration honest - and if you want to learn the recipes, there's a market-to-table cooking class for that.

Practical Notes

Ubud sits higher than the coast, so it's a touch cooler and catches more rain - mornings are reliably the best part of the day, and a light rain layer earns its place in your bag. Ride-hailing apps are restricted in parts of town, so for day trips a private driver is the practical choice. The centre gets congested from late morning; do your walking early, your museums and cafes in the heat of the day, and your dance performance after dark.

A Good First Day

Here is a shape that works, tested on many first-time guests. Start at the art market as it opens, before the heat and the crowds, then cross to the palace for twenty minutes of carved gates and courtyards. Walk Monkey Forest Road south, browsing as you go, and give the Monkey Forest itself a relaxed hour. Take lunch somewhere with a paddy view, then spend the hot early afternoon in a museum or back at the pool. Around four, head out to the Campuhan Ridge for the soft light, and finish with a dance performance near the palace at seven. Nothing rushed, everything close.

Day two is when Ubud earns its reputation as a base: sunrise at Mount Batur, the temples and terraces to the north, or the craft villages south - each a comfortable loop that ends back at your own bed.

When to Come

Ubud works year-round. In the dry season (April-October) the walking trails are at their best; in the wet months the valleys turn a deeper green and the rain mostly keeps to the afternoons - fine timing for a museum, a massage, or a long lunch. The town is busiest in July, August, and around New Year, when the centre's narrow streets genuinely clog; come early in the day and the town is yours. For the full picture, see our month-by-month guide.

Where to Stay

Location changes the Ubud you get. In the centre you can walk to everything - and hear everything; light sleepers should ask for a room off the main roads. Penestanan and Campuhan, across the river, trade walkability for quiet and green views, still ten minutes from town. Further out, villages like Sayan and Kedewatan on the Ayung river gorge, or the lanes north of town put you alone among the paddies, where mornings start with roosters instead of scooters - the classic Ubud villa experience. Wherever you stay, factor the drive: a villa twenty minutes out is bliss at sunrise and a consideration at dinner time.

Good to Know

  • Best base for culture, countryside & day trips island-wide
  • Walkable centre - palace, market & Monkey Forest close together
  • Mornings are the golden hours: cooler, quieter, better light
  • Cooler and wetter than the coast - pack a light layer
  • Ubud Kecak: Tuesdays & Sundays, 7 PM
