Temple Etiquette & Dress Code in Bali

How to visit Bali's temples respectfully - sarongs, sashes, and simple do's.

Bali Guide › People & Culture

Bali's temples are active places of worship, and visiting them respectfully is simple once you know the basics. The main rule is modest dress: a sarong and sash tied around the waist, with shoulders covered.

At major temples these are usually available to borrow or rent, and on our tours we arrange them for you. Beyond dress, keep your voice low near prayers, don't point your feet at shrines, and don't climb on the structures for photos.

By tradition, women who are menstruating are asked not to enter temple grounds. When in doubt, follow your guide's lead - a little courtesy is always appreciated.

A Few More Courtesies

Behaviour matters as much as dress. Walk quietly, keep behind anyone who is praying, and never place yourself higher than a shrine for a photo. Hats and sunglasses come off inside temple courtyards, and flash photography during prayers is best avoided.

Offerings are everywhere, including on the ground - take care not to step on them. A calm, unhurried manner is the norm, and if you're ever unsure, a quiet glance at what locals are doing, or a word with your guide, will keep you on the right side of custom.

Good to Know

  • Wear a sarong & sash; cover your shoulders
  • Usually provided at big temples (and on our tours)
  • Follow your guide near prayers & shrines
