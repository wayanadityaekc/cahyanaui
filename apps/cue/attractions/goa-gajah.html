Goa Gajah - The Elephant Cave

A 9th-century cave sanctuary with a demon-carved entrance, just outside Ubud.

The Experience

The Cave

Through the Demon's Mouth

The heart of Goa Gajah is the cave itself, its mouth carved into a monstrous face you step straight through. Inside, a narrow T-shaped passage leads to ancient meditation niches cut into the rock and weathered shrines lit by oil lamps. Dating to around the 9th century, it's one of Ubud's oldest and most atmospheric sites - cool, dim, and heavy with age.

The Bathing Pools

Ancient Fountains & Courtyards

Just outside the cave lie stone bathing pools, fed by fountains held by carved figures, rediscovered and excavated last century. Around them stretch shaded courtyards scattered with statues and fragments of old shrines. The mix of Hindu and Buddhist relics here hints at Bali's layered past, when different faiths shared the same sacred ground.

The Ravine

Jungle Paths to Hidden Shrines

Behind the main courtyard, mossy steps drop down into a green ravine, past streams, rice fields, and weathered carvings half-swallowed by the jungle. It's shaded and quiet - pleasant even in the heat - and easy to see why pilgrims have been drawn here for over a thousand years. Take the loop slowly and let the sanctuary reveal itself.
