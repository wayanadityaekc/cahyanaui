The Best Waterfalls in Bali

From easy jungle pools to wild, multi-tier treks.

Bali Guide › Nature

Bali's volcanic interior is stacked with waterfalls - by some counts well over a hundred, from roadside cascades ten minutes from a car park to jungle falls that ask for a real trek. They're one of the island's best free-ish pleasures (most charge a small entrance fee) and one of the easiest things to get wrong: pick the wrong ones to combine and you spend the day driving; skip the footwear advice and the stairs will collect their tax. Here's how to do waterfalls well.

Near Ubud: the Easy Wins

Tegenungan is the classic starter - twenty minutes from Ubud, a wide, powerful curtain of water with a swimmable pool, warungs at the top, and a manageable stairway. It's popular for those exact reasons, so come early. Around Ubud you'll also find smaller canyon falls like Kanto Lampo and Tibumana, where the water slides down rock steps shallow enough to wade - good with kids, busiest with photographers. East of Ubud, Tukad Cepung is the famous one for light: a fall inside a canyon where sunbeams drop through the opening mid-morning on clear days. No beam is guaranteed - that's sunlight, not a light switch.

The Northern Belt: the Big Ones

The serious waterfalls live in the hills of north Bali, and they cluster well enough to make a full day of it. Banyumala is a twin fall spilling down a mossy wall into one of the clearest swimming pools on the island - the short, steep trail in keeps the crowds down. The Munduk area hides several falls along its ridge walks, cooler and mistier than the lowlands. Gitgit is the accessible veteran on the Singaraja road. Our North Bali Tour strings the best of this belt into one day with the Twin Lakes viewpoint on the way.

And then there's Sekumpul - often called Bali's most beautiful waterfall, and the one that asks the most: a proper trek down valley stairs and across a small river to reach a bowl of jungle where several tall falls drop at once. It's the anchor of our Lovina Dolphin & Sekumpul day, and the effort is precisely why it stays uncrowded.

When to Go

Waterfalls run on rain, so the wet season (November-March) is when they're at full power - louder, wider, and photogenically misty. The cost: trails are muddiest then, and after heavy rain some pools run brown and swimming may be closed for safety. In the dry season the falls are gentler but the water clearer and the paths easier. Either way, the golden rule is go in the morning: fewer people, kinder light, and at falls like Tukad Cepung the light show is strictly a mid-morning event.

What to Bring (and Expect)

  • Shoes with grip - every waterfall involves stairs or a trail, usually wet
  • Swimsuit worn under clothes + towel - changing rooms are basic where they exist
  • A dry bag for phone & wallet; small cash for entrance fees
  • Water and a snack for the treks - Sekumpul especially
  • Respect the signs: some falls are sacred and swimming is not allowed

One honest warning that saves knees: what goes down must come back up. The beautiful ones sit in valleys, and the return stairway in midday heat is the part nobody photographs. Go early, take it slow, and let your driver carry the schedule - on our waterfall days the pacing is built in.

Which Waterfall Day Fits You

  • Easy half-day near Ubud - Tegenungan plus the canyon falls, part of the Ubud Tour
  • The full northern day - Banyumala, Munduk & Gitgit with the Twin Lakes viewpoint: the North Bali Tour
  • The trek day - Sekumpul, paired with the dawn dolphins: Lovina & Sekumpul
  • With small kids or tired knees - stick to Tegenungan & Tibumana; skip Sekumpul

A note on photos: the classic waterfall shot needs a tripod-steady hand or a rock to lean on, and the spray reaches further than you think - the dry bag earns its keep here. Early morning gives still air and even light; by midday the contrast between jungle shade and bright sky fights every camera. And when a fall is busy, patience beats elbows: most crowds move through in waves, and ten quiet minutes usually arrive if you wait for them.

Swimming Safely

Waterfall pools look tame and mostly are - but a few rules keep them that way. Never swim directly under a big fall: the falling water and the churn beneath it are far stronger than they look. Enter feet first every time; rocks move between seasons and depth is deceptive in cloudy water. After heavy rain, skip swimming entirely - flash flow is the one real danger at Bali's falls, and local guides close pools for good reason. If a rope, a sign, or a guide says no, that's the answer.

Good to Know

  • Small entrance fee at most falls - carry cash
  • Wet season = biggest flow; dry season = clearest pools
  • Mornings: fewer people, better light, cooler stairs
  • Grippy shoes & a dry bag are the two non-negotiables
  • Sacred falls may not allow swimming - follow the signs
