Jatiluwih Rice Terraces

Jatiluwih is a broad expanse of rice terraces on the slopes below Mount Batukaru in West Bali, part of a UNESCO World Heritage landscape. It is still farmed the traditional subak way, with walking trails running through the paddies.

About Jatiluwih

The Terraces

A UNESCO Sea of Green

Jatiluwih is a UNESCO World Heritage landscape of rice terraces spreading across the slopes below Mount Batukaru. Many travellers find it more impressive than Tegalalang: far larger and far quieter, with layered greens rolling away in every direction. There is no single viewpoint, just fields running to the horizon, and the scale is what stays with you.

The Trails

Walk Into the Paddies

Marked walking trails run right through the paddies, so you can step into the landscape rather than view it from the road. Follow the paths between the flooded fields, past farmers at work and the occasional shrine, at whatever pace suits you. The quiet out among the terraces is part of the appeal, and it is one of Bali's more peaceful stops.

The Subak

A Thousand-Year Water Culture

Jatiluwih is still farmed the traditional subak way, Bali's communal irrigation system, in continuous use for over a thousand years and central to its UNESCO listing. Water is shared field to field through hand-cut channels, guided by the temples and the farming calendar. It is a working landscape rather than a manicured attraction, which is much of the point.
