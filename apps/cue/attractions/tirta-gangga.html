Tirta Gangga Water Garden

Built by the last king of Karangasem, Tirta Gangga is a water garden of tiered fountains, koi ponds, and stepping stones set across the main pond. It sits in the hills of East Bali and stays green and quiet away from the crowds.

About Tirta Gangga

The Water Garden

A Garden Built by Karangasem's Kings

Tirta Gangga was built in 1946 by the last raja of Karangasem, around a natural spring the royal family held sacred. Much of it was destroyed by the 1963 eruption of Mount Agung and later rebuilt, and it still carries a calm, regal feel. The name means "water of the Ganges", a nod to the Hindu holy river.

The Royal Garden

Fountains and Tiered Pools

Tiered fountains bubble and spill between manicured lawns, stone statues, and ornamental ponds that roll toward the hills behind. A tall tiered fountain stands at the centre, and the whole garden is green, peaceful, and easy to photograph. It is one of East Bali's quieter, less-crowded stops.

The Stepping Stones & Koi

Walk Across the Water

A row of stepping stones is set into the main pond, letting you pick your way across the water between the koi; it is the shot most people come for. The ponds are full of large, well-fed fish, and food is usually on sale to feed them, a hit with kids. Take the stones slowly, since they can be slick, and morning is the calmest, coolest time to wander.
