Getting Around Bali

Drivers, charters, scooters - and how to plan realistic days.

Bali Guide › Good to Know

Bali has no train network, and its roads are winding and often congested, so how you get around matters. For most travellers, a private driver or car charter is the most comfortable option - especially for temple runs and countryside days.

Scooters are cheap and everywhere, but Bali's traffic is genuinely risky for the inexperienced, and an International Driving Permit is required. Ride-hailing apps work in some areas but are restricted in others.

Because journeys take longer than the map suggests, it's best to plan realistic days rather than cramming. We offer private airport transfers and full-day charters with a local driver.

Getting From A to B

Distances look small on a map but take longer in reality, thanks to narrow, winding roads and steady traffic around the south and Ubud. As a rough guide, allow more time than the kilometres suggest, and try not to pack too many far-flung stops into one day.

From the airport, a pre-booked private transfer is the smoothest arrival, with a driver waiting rather than haggling at the kerb. For sightseeing, a full-day charter with a local driver lets you cover more, comfortably, while someone else handles the traffic and parking.
