Bali's Beaches & Surf

Where to swim, surf, and catch the sunset around the island.

Bali Guide › What to Do

Bali's coastline changes character completely as you move around the island, and knowing which coast does what saves you from the classic mistake: expecting one beach to be all things. The short version - the south has the white sand and the surf, the Bukit has the cliff-backed coves, the west is black-sand surf country, the east is calm and good for snorkeling, and the north is quiet black sand with dolphins at dawn. Here's how to choose.

Coast by Coast

The southern resorts (Kuta-Legian-Seminyak). Long, wide, golden-sand beaches with gentle beach breaks - this is where Bali surfing began and where most people still learn. Busy, commercial, and convenient; sunset here comes with a crowd and a drink menu.

The Bukit Peninsula. A different world: white coves at the foot of limestone cliffs, reached by stairways. Padang Padang is the famous small one, Melasti the easy, clean one, Pandawa the family one. The prettiest swimming water in south Bali - full detail in our Uluwatu & Bukit guide.

The west coast (Canggu to Tanah Lot). Volcanic black sand and consistent beach breaks - surf-first territory with a big sunset culture. Swim between the flags; the rips are no joke. More in the Canggu guide.

The east coast (Sanur to Amed). The calm side. Sanur's lagoon is protected by reef - flat, shallow, and the best bet for small children and easy swimming. Further north, Candidasa and the black-sand bays of Amed are quiet, and the snorkeling gets genuinely good - coral, fish, and in Amed's case a famous shipwreck within swimming distance. Our east-coast snorkeling stop is built around this.

The north coast (Lovina). Calm, dark sand, and known for one thing above all: the dawn dolphin boats. A different, slower Bali - nobody comes here for the beach itself, and that's part of the appeal.

Surfing: Where You Belong

Never surfed? Book a lesson on the sandy beach breaks of Kuta, Legian, or Canggu. The whitewater is forgiving, boards are soft-tops, and standing up on day one is normal. Improving? The peaks along Canggu and Berawa give you real unbroken waves without a coral floor. Experienced? The reef breaks of the Bukit - Uluwatu, Padang Padang, Bingin - are the reason Bali is on the world surf map, and they demand the skills to match: shallow reef, strong currents, and crowded line-ups that know what they're doing.

Season matters too: the west and Bukit coasts get their best, cleanest waves in the dry season (roughly April-October), while the east coast works better in the wet months. Whatever your level, the ocean here deserves respect - swim and surf between the flags where lifeguards operate, and if the locals aren't going in, don't go in.

Picking by Situation

  • With small kids - Sanur's calm lagoon, or Pandawa with its sunbeds and warungs
  • For the photo - the Bukit coves: cliffs, clear water, white sand
  • To learn surfing - Kuta or Canggu beach breaks, morning lessons
  • To snorkel - the east coast: Blue Lagoon & Amed
  • For quiet - the east and north coasts, any weekday
  • Watersports (jet ski, parasail, banana boat) - Tanjung Benoa, the dedicated strip

Practical Notes

Beach days from Ubud are day trips - the coast is roughly 1-2 hours away depending on which one, so pick a single coast per day. Bring reef shoes for the rockier eastern shores, cash for beach warungs and sunbeds, and sun cover: shade is commercial on the busy beaches and absent on the quiet ones. The light for photos is best in the last two hours of the day on the west and south coasts - which is exactly when our Bali Hidden Beaches and South Bali & Sunset Kecak days put you there.

The Nusa Islands

The photo at the top of this page - the T-rex-shaped cliff of Kelingking - is not on mainland Bali. It sits on Nusa Penida, one of three islands a 30-45 minute fast boat from Sanur, and it belongs on this page because so many guests arrive expecting to find it on the beach ring road. Penida is a full, long day: rough roads, big distances, and viewpoints rather than easy swimming - the famous beaches there are mostly admired from above. Neighbouring Nusa Lembongan is the gentler pick, with calm bays and easy snorkeling. Treat the Nusas as their own day trip with an early start, not an add-on to a mainland beach day - tell us your dates and we will arrange the crossing and the island car.

Sunsets, Ranked

  • Tanah Lot - the temple-on-a-rock silhouette; the classic photo
  • Uluwatu - cliffs, surf below, and the Kecak fire dance as the sun drops
  • Canggu - casual: beanbag, drink, surfers in the frame
  • Jimbaran - sunset with grilled seafood on the sand
  • Sanur - the exception: come at sunrise, not sunset (it faces east)

Good to Know

  • South & Bukit = white sand; west & north = black volcanic sand
  • Calmest swimming: Sanur lagoon & the east coast
  • Rips are real on surf coasts - swim between the flags
  • West coast surf peaks in dry season; east coast in the wet
  • One coast per day from Ubud - they're further apart than they look
