Ubud Tour

Bali's cultural heart in one unforgettable day - rice terraces and a coffee plantation, the swing, sacred temples at Tirta Empul and Gunung Kawi, the Goa Gajah cave, Tegenungan waterfall, and the famous Monkey Forest. A private car, a local driver, your pace.

What You'll Visit

Stop 1

Tegalalang Rice Terrace & Coffee Plantation

We start at the iconic stepped rice fields north of Ubud, hand-carved over generations and still fed by Bali's thousand year old subak irrigation system. Right beside it, walk through a working plantation to see how coffee grows and taste the famous kopi luwak straight from the source. Tastings are complimentary.

Stop 2

Swing

Right at the plantation, strap into a harness and soar out over the jungle valley on Bali's famous swing - nothing below you but treetops and the rice fields far down. Choose a gentle low swing or the high one that flings you well out over the gorge. Optional, and worth it for the photo alone.

Stop 3

Tirta Empul Holy Water Temple

Bali's most sacred spring temple, where Balinese Hindus have performed the melukat purification ritual for over a thousand years. You're welcome to join the ritual yourself - we'll arrange a sarong and walk you through the etiquette so you can take part with confidence and respect.

Stop 4

Gunung Kawi Temple

An 11th-century wonder carved directly into the rock face of a river valley. Ten giant shrines, each cut from the living cliff, reached by a staircase down through emerald rice paddies. It's quieter than the famous temples and genuinely breathtaking. A little walking, a lot of reward.

Stop 5

Goa Gajah - The Elephant Cave

A 9th-century sanctuary whose cave mouth is carved into a monstrous demon face you step straight through. Inside are ancient meditation niches; outside, bathing pools and jungle paths lead down to hidden shrines. One of Ubud's oldest and most mysterious sites.

Stop 6

Tegenungan Waterfall

A wide, powerful waterfall tucked into the jungle just outside Ubud, with a natural pool at its base you can swim in (though sometimes not, if the water is too high). It's one of the few major Bali waterfalls with easy access - a short walk down, and a genuinely refreshing reward after a morning of temples.

Stop 7

Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary

We finish in the heart of Ubud at a moss-covered jungle temple complex, home to more than 700 long-tailed macaques. Walk beneath ancient banyan roots and past weathered stone guardians in one of Bali's most atmospheric sanctuaries. Your guide will show you how to keep your belongings safe and interact with the monkeys respectfully.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this a private tour?

Yes - every departure is 100% private: your own air-conditioned car with your own friendly English-speaking driver, never shared with other guests. The price is per car for up to 5 people; groups of 6 or more simply take a second car.

Are entrance tickets included in the price?

You choose between two versions with the Standard / Exclusive toggle above. Standard covers the private car, petrol, and your crew - entrance tickets are paid at each stop, so you only pay for the places you actually enter. Exclusive adds entrance tickets for all the listed attractions for everyone in your group, so the whole day is prepaid and you never queue at a ticket window.

How long is the tour, and when does it start?

Plan for up to 8 hours door to door. Since the car is yours, the start time is up to you - most guests leave Ubud in the morning, and we agree the exact pick-up time when you book.

Can we change or skip stops?

Absolutely - the listed stops are a starting point, not a fixed route. Add a stop, swap one out, or stay longer wherever you're enjoying yourself; just tell us before the day or tell your driver on the road.

How do I book, and what do I pay upfront?

Tap the Book button, pick your date, and confirm the details with us on WhatsApp. Only a 10% deposit secures your car and driver - the rest is settled later, so your plans stay flexible.

What should I bring?

Comfortable shoes, sunscreen, and a camera cover most of it. Bring swimwear and a change of clothes if you'd like to swim at Tegenungan or join the purification bathing at Tirta Empul, and a sarong is easy to rent at the temples that require one.
