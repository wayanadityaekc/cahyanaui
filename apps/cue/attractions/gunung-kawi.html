Gunung Kawi Temple

An 11th-century shrine complex carved into a river-valley cliff, reached through the rice fields.

The Experience

The Shrines

Ten Memorials Cut From the Cliff

Gunung Kawi's ten giant shrines are carved directly into the rock face of the Pakerisan river valley - each candi nearly ten metres tall and close to a thousand years old. Cut from the living cliff rather than built, they're among the oldest and largest monuments in Bali, believed to honour an 11th-century king and his family.

The Walk Down

Through the Rice Paddies to the River

You reach the shrines on a stone stairway - around 300 steps - that winds down through emerald rice terraces to the valley floor. The descent is half the experience: the paddies, the village gardens, and the sound of the sacred river growing louder as you go. Take it slowly, and the climb back up is easier than it sounds.

The Atmosphere

Bali Before the Crowds

Gunung Kawi stays far quieter than Bali's famous temples, and that's its magic. Standing at the foot of the carved cliffs with the sacred river running past, it genuinely feels like stepping back a thousand years - often with the valley almost to yourself.
