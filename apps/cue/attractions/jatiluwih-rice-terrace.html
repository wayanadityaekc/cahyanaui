Jatiluwih Rice Terraces

A UNESCO World Heritage sea of green terraces, farmed the traditional subak way.

The Experience

The Terraces

A UNESCO Sea of Green

Jatiluwih is a UNESCO World Heritage landscape of rice terraces stretching to the horizon on the slopes below Mount Batukaru. Many travellers find it even more impressive than Tegalalang - far larger and far quieter, with layered greens rolling away in every direction. The sheer scale is what stays with you; there's no single viewpoint, just fields as far as you can see.

The Trails

Walk Into the Paddies

Marked walking trails wind right through the paddies, so you can step into the landscape rather than just view it from the road. Follow the paths between the flooded fields, past farmers at work and the odd shrine, at whatever pace suits you. The silence out among the terraces is part of the draw - it's one of Bali's most peaceful stops.

The Subak

A Thousand-Year Water Culture

Jatiluwih is still farmed the traditional subak way - Bali's communal irrigation system, in continuous use for over a thousand years and central to its UNESCO listing. Water is shared field to field through hand-cut channels, guided by the temples and the farming calendar. It's a living, working landscape, not a manicured attraction, and that's exactly what makes it special.
