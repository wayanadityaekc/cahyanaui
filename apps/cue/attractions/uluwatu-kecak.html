Uluwatu Kecak Fire Dance

The Kecak is a Balinese dance told entirely by voice - more than seventy men chanting in interlocking rhythm. At Uluwatu it's staged on a clifftop above the Indian Ocean, every evening as the sun goes down.

About the Kecak

The Performance

Seventy Voices and a Ring of Fire

The Kecak uses no instruments - just a circle of bare-chested men chanting "cak-cak-cak" in an interlocking rhythm while the story of the Ramayana unfolds: Rama, Sita, the demon king Rawana, and Hanoman the white monkey, who ends the show leaping through flames. The form took shape in the 1930s, built on an older trance ritual.

The Setting

A Clifftop Stage at Sunset

The open-air amphitheatre stands beside Uluwatu temple, about 70 metres above the surf. As the chanting builds, the sky turns gold and the sun drops into the ocean behind the dancers - the reason this is the best-known Kecak on the island. Seating is stone tiers, open to the sky and facing straight out to sea.

Visiting

Performed Every Evening

Unlike the Ubud Kecak, which runs only on certain nights, the Uluwatu show is on every day at sunset, and it's popular, so arriving early gets you the better cliff-side seats. The stage sits in temple grounds where the resident monkeys are known to snatch sunglasses and phones, so keep small items zipped away.
