Taman Ayun Royal Temple

The royal temple of the Mengwi kingdom, ringed by a wide moat with rows of tiered meru towers.

The Experience

The Meru Towers

Rows of Tiered Shrines

The inner courtyard of Taman Ayun is lined with meru - multi-tiered shrines with thatched black roofs stacked in odd numbers, rising in neat rows toward the sky. Each tier honours a mountain, a deity or an ancestor of the Mengwi royal house, and seen together across the temple grounds they form one of the most photographed skylines in Bali.

The Moat & Gardens

A Temple on Its Own Island

The main sanctuary sits on an island encircled by a wide moat, so the temple appears to float on the water. You enter through a split gate (candi bentar) and follow manicured lawns and shaded walkways around the grounds - the layout, the water and the gardens together give Taman Ayun a calm, garden-park feel unlike any other temple on the island.

Royal & UNESCO Heritage

Built by the Mengwi Kings

Taman Ayun was founded by the royal family of Mengwi in the 1600s as the kingdom's main state temple. Today it is part of Bali's UNESCO-listed cultural landscape tied to the traditional subak irrigation system - a reminder that its beauty is rooted in the same water and rice culture that shapes the island.
