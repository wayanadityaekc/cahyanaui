East Bali Tour

The wild, regal side of the island — royal water palaces, the Gates of Heaven at Lempuyang, Bali's holiest temple on the slopes of Mount Agung, and rice valleys that look untouched by time.

What You'll Visit

Stop 1

Lempuyang Temple — Gates of Heaven

Bali's most photographed split gate, framing Mount Agung on a clear morning. We arrive early to beat the queue, and your guide will handle the ticket, sarong, and the famous mirror photo so you don't waste half the day waiting in line.

Stop 2

Tirta Gangga Water Palace

A former royal garden built around tiered fountains and koi-filled pools, with stepping stones you can walk across the water on. Peaceful, green, and endlessly photogenic — one of East Bali's most underrated stops.

Stop 3

Taman Ujung Water Palace

The last king of Karangasem's seaside palace, half European, half Balinese, with wide reflecting pools and views out to the ocean and up to Mount Agung. Quiet, breezy, and rarely crowded.

Stop 4

Besakih — The Mother Temple

Bali's largest and holiest temple complex, built in terraces up the slope of Mount Agung. More than 20 temples in one sanctuary, still the spiritual centre of the island after a thousand years. Sarong and guide included.

Stop 5

Sidemen Valley

What Ubud looked like thirty years ago — a wide green valley of rice terraces and small villages, with Mount Agung towering behind. We stop for the view, and for a coffee at a warung where locals actually eat.

Stop 6

Tenganan — Ancient Bali Aga Village

One of Bali's original pre-Hindu villages, where families still weave the rare double-ikat gringsing cloth by hand — a technique found in only three places on earth. A rare look at Bali before Bali became famous.

Stop 7

Tukad Cepung Waterfall

A waterfall hidden inside a cave, where beams of light drop through the opening above and land on the water. Come around midday and the light is unreal. A short walk through a river gorge to get there — worth every step.
