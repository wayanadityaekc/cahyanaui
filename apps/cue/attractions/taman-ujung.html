Taman Ujung Water Palace

The grand water palace of the Karangasem kings - ornate pavilions, wide reflecting ponds, and bridges set against Mount Agung and the sea.

The Experience

The Water Palace

Ponds, Bridges & Pavilions

Built by the last raja of Karangasem in the early 1900s, Taman Ujung (Taman Soekasada) spreads across wide reflecting ponds linked by ornate bridges and European-Balinese pavilions. It's grand, symmetrical, and endlessly photogenic.

The Setting

Framed by Agung & the Sea

On a clear day the pools mirror Mount Agung on one side and the ocean on the other, with manicured gardens climbing the hillside. A short walk up the terraces gives you the postcard view over the whole palace.

Good to Know

An Easy, Elegant Stop

Flat, easy walking with plenty of shade and photo spots - allow around 45 minutes. It sits close to Tirta Gangga, so the two royal water palaces pair naturally on an East Bali day.

Highlights

  • Wide reflecting ponds linked by ornate bridges
  • European-Balinese royal pavilions of the Karangasem kings
  • Views framed by Mount Agung and the sea on clear days
  • Pairs naturally with the nearby Tirta Gangga water palace

Before You Go

  • Flat, easy walking around the ponds and terraces
  • Late morning light is best over the water
  • Bring a hat and water - shade is limited on the terraces
  • Modest dress appreciated at this royal heritage site
