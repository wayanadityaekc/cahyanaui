Gunung Kawi Temple

An 11th-century shrine complex carved into a river-valley cliff, reached through the rice fields.

About

Gunung Kawi is one of Bali's oldest and most striking ancient sites - ten towering shrines (candi) cut directly into the rock face of the Pakerisan river valley, nearly a thousand years old. A stone stairway leads down through emerald rice paddies to the temple, where the giant memorials stand carved from the living cliff on either side of the sacred river.

It's quieter than Bali's famous temples, and that's part of the magic. The walk down past the terraces, the sound of the river, and the sheer scale of the shrines make it feel like stepping back a thousand years - and you'll often have the valley almost to yourself.

Highlights

  • Ten giant shrines carved into the living cliff (11th century)
  • Scenic stairway down through terraced rice paddies
  • Set along the sacred Pakerisan river
  • Quieter and less crowded than Bali's famous temples

Good to Know

  • Around 300 steps down and back up - take your time
  • Sarong required and provided (we arrange it)
  • Wear comfortable shoes; can be warm at midday
