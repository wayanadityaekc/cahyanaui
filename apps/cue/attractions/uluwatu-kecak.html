Uluwatu Kecak Fire Dance

Bali's most dramatic performance in Bali's most dramatic setting - more than seventy chanting voices, a ring of fire, and the sun sinking into the Indian Ocean behind the clifftop stage. Every evening at sunset.

The Experience

The Performance

Seventy Voices and a Ring of Fire

No instruments - just a circle of bare-chested men chanting "cak-cak-cak" in an interlocking rhythm as the story of the Ramayana unfolds: Rama, Sita, the demon king Rawana, and Hanoman the white monkey, who ends the show leaping through flames.

The Setting

A Clifftop Amphitheatre at Sunset

The open-air amphitheatre sits beside Uluwatu temple, 70 metres above the crashing surf. As the performance builds, the sky turns gold and the sun drops straight into the ocean behind the dancers - the reason this is the most famous Kecak in Bali.

Good to Know

Performed Every Evening

Unlike the Ubud Kecak (Tuesdays & Sundays), the Uluwatu show runs every day at sunset - and it's popular, so arriving early gets you the best cliff-side seats. We time the whole South Coast day so you walk in relaxed, with the temple visit done first.
