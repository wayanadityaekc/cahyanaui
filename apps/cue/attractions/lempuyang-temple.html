Lempuyang Temple - Gates of Heaven

Bali's most photographed split gate, framing sacred Mount Agung on a clear morning.

The Experience

The Gate

The Famous Gates of Heaven

The split gate (candi bentar) at the temple entrance is one of Bali's most photographed sights, its two halves perfectly framing sacred Mount Agung in the distance. On a clear morning the mountain rises straight through the gap. Your guide handles the ticket, the sarong, and the well-known mirror-reflection photo so you don't lose half the day waiting in line.

The Temple

One of Bali's Great Directional Temples

Pura Lempuyang Luhur is one of the island's oldest and highest temples, part of the ring of great directional sanctuaries that guard Bali. Perched high on the mountainside and wrapped in cool air and forest, it is a serious place of worship far beyond the viral photo. The gate you see at the entrance is only the first of many that climb toward the summit shrine.

The Morning

Arrive Early, Beat the Crowd

We arrive at first light, when the air is cool, the queue is short, and Agung is most likely to be clear of cloud. Standing at the gate as the mountain emerges from the morning haze is the kind of moment that feels earned - and far calmer than the midday crush. It is a high, breezy spot, so a light layer and a clear sky make all the difference.

  • Go early - the photo queue gets very long by mid-morning

  • It's high and cool - bring a light layer; the view needs a clear sky
  • The "reflection" is made with a mirror under the phone, not water
