Bali Money, SIM Cards & Visas

Currency, connectivity, and entry basics before you go.

Bali Guide › Good to Know

A few practical basics make arriving in Bali smoother. The local currency is the Indonesian rupiah (IDR); it's worth carrying some cash for warungs, markets, and small shops, while cards and ATMs are common in tourist areas.

For staying connected, a local SIM card or eSIM is inexpensive and coverage is good across most of the island - handy for maps, ride-hailing, and messaging.

Entry rules vary by nationality; many visitors can obtain a visa on arrival, but requirements change, so always check the latest official Indonesian immigration guidance for your country before you travel.

Arriving Prepared

A little planning smooths the first day. Withdraw or exchange some rupiah for small purchases, and use ATMs attached to banks where possible; card payments are widely accepted in restaurants, hotels, and larger shops, though smaller places may add a card-machine surcharge.

An eSIM you set up before landing means you're connected the moment you arrive, though local SIM cards are cheap to buy at the airport too. Visa rules change from time to time and differ by nationality, so treat any guide as a starting point and confirm the current official requirements before you fly.

Good to Know

  • Currency: Indonesian rupiah (IDR); carry some cash
  • Local SIM/eSIM = cheap, good coverage
  • Check current visa rules for your country before you go
